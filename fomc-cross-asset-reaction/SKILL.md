# FOMC cross-asset reaction

For press-conference summaries, follow references/press-conference-tracking.md to retrieve actual Q&A and distinguish it from premeeting commentary. For every FOMC research task, read the existing longitudinal tracker and update it after analysis using the same reference, so changes in the Fed’s stance remain comparable across meetings. Apply the market steps below only when market reaction is requested or included; do not expand a conference-summary request into a full market recap.

1. Call session_status to establish the current time in America/Los_Angeles and America/New_York; state whether the answer covers the statement, press conference, or close so later information is not attributed to an earlier window.
2. Fetch https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm with web_fetch to identify the current and previous meeting dates. Fetch each statement using https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetaryYYYYMMDDa.htm and the current implementation note at monetaryYYYYMMDDa1.htm. Verify the date and release time inside each document before extracting numbers.
3. Compare the target range, vote count, dissent direction, growth wording, employment assessment, inflation language, and forward guidance against the previous statement. Quote only changed phrases and distinguish a pause from a promise that the tightening cycle has ended. Read the implementation note before calling reserve-management purchases QE; operational reserve maintenance does not by itself establish a new easing program.
4. Retrieve market coverage with web_search queries such as "<Month day year> Fed decision Treasury yields stocks" and "site.cnbc.com/<YYYY>/<MM>/<DD> us treasurys bonds yields federal reserve". Fetch the matched pages and check that their bodies actually include the released decision, because indexed headlines may still describe the predecision session. For equity live blogs, include the preceding calendar date in discovery because the next session's blog may use that date in its URL.
5. Record 2Y, 10Y, and 30Y yields, equity index direction, the reported timestamp, and the change baseline separately. Label previous-close changes as daily changes; only call a move a post-decision change when comparable observations immediately before and after the release are available. Do not use opening equity gains or daily Treasury/FRED observations as substitutes for the event window. If a headline only verifies that stocks remained higher, report that limited fact without inventing the size or claiming an announcement-driven rally.
6. Record any FedWatch probability with its source and observation time. Compare probabilities across matching contracts and windows; do not describe a prior-day versus current difference as a release-minute repricing.
7. Compare yield changes across maturities before classifying the curve move. Treat similar 2Y and 10Y increases as broadly parallel rather than automatically blaming long-end fiscal risk. Use synchronized TIPS real yields and breakevens before quantifying inflation versus real-rate contributions; mark the decomposition unavailable if those data are missing.
8. Explain equities through both earnings expectations and discount rates. Label explanations as interpretations, and identify concurrent earnings or other catalysts before assigning the entire move to the Fed. Interpret stocks up with yields up as distinct from a duration-led easing rally.
9. Deliver a Chinese conclusion-first brief with the as-of time, verified decision changes, yield/equity snapshot with baselines, interpretation, and concrete confirmation or invalidation signals. Use bullets rather than tables in Discord. Cite the Fed statement and fetched market sources; explicitly identify missing minute-level prices and avoid presenting a preconference snapshot as the final reaction.

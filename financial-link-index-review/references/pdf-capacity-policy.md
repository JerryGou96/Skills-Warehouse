Run `pdfinfo "$PDF"` to obtain the page count, then `pdftotext -layout "$PDF" "$TEXT"` through an available execution tool. Read every page in bounded ranges, retaining form-feed page boundaries and a page coverage checklist. Treat PDF creation metadata as distinct from the article publication date.
2. Render chart-bearing pages with `pdftoppm -f FIRST -l LAST -scale-to 1800 -png "$PDF" "$PREFIX"`; open each resulting image with `view_image` and log the page actually inspected. Rendering alone is not visual verification. Increase resolution for small labels rather than guessing their values. Preserve locked, redacted, or unreadable cells as unavailable; do not reconstruct them from bar lengths or contextual expectations.
3. Create a figure ledger with metric, value, unit, forecast year, geography/sample, construction stage, source page, classification and caveat. Separate global additions, national additions, selected-state totals, cumulative pipeline and installed capacity; verify the denominator before calculating shares or growth. Record whether GW means IT load, facility input power or contracted power; if unspecified, retain that uncertainty instead of translating it into chips or revenue.
4. Keep planned, under-construction, operational, policy-exposed, delayed and cancelled capacity in separate fields. Label election sweeps and best/worst outcomes as scenarios rather than probability-weighted forecasts. Do not apply a delay ratio from an older sample to a newly expanded policy scope without project-level evidence.
5. Read maps, tables, prose and footnotes against each other. Preserve exact chart values alongside rounded prose, and label arithmetic using rounded inputs approximate. Distinguish statewide annual additions from a named campus multi-year commitment even when the numbers coincide; distinguish demand curtailment from generation capacity. Treat the order of a state list as non-ranking unless explicitly established.
6. Classify each restriction by instrument and authority: construction moratorium, permit-processing hold, new tax-exemption freeze, repeal proposal, interconnection audit or utility tariff. Record whether it is enacted, proposed, campaign language or an author assumption. Separate existing permits and exemptions from future phases; a building under construction can still lack approval for its next power stage.
7. Build a milestone ledger separating audit delivery, agency reopening, legislation passage, effective date, technical review, permit issuance and energization. Treat an announced expiry or report date as a milestone, not a guaranteed approval date. Assess delay only when a required approval lies on the project critical path and the hold exceeds schedule slack. Apply the same test to behind-the-meter generation, which still needs relevant environmental and land approvals.
8. Split economic effects into recurring costs, upfront infrastructure payments, collateral/security, tax changes and schedule delay. Explain that ability to finance higher costs does not guarantee timely permits, equipment or power.
9. Audit candidate coding against source caveats before repeating aggregate political counts. Preserve no-position-found cases and distinguish transparency pledges from explicit NDA bans. Label polling with its cutoff and distinguish state legislatures from the federal Senate, governor authority from utility commissions, and campaign promises from binding rules.

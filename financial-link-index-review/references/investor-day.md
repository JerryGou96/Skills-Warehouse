# Investor Day and long-range guidance summaries

1. Search with web_search for issuer name, event date, and “Investor Day” or “Financial Analyst Meeting”; use web_fetch on the issuer event page and the dated company release, because a live commentary fragment does not establish the complete guidance. If the IR page is unreadable, search the exact release title on the official newsroom or PRNewswire and verify the issuer attribution and publication date before treating the syndicated release as primary evidence.
2. Follow presentation and replay links from the release; record which documents and Q&A were actually read, because a press-release summary is not a complete meeting recap. Summarize verified release content when decks remain unavailable and list the missing operating details rather than inventing them.
3. Inspect embedded company exhibits in supplied broker PDFs before building the guidance ledger, because extracted prose can omit chart values and footnote definitions. Locate the actual attachment, run `pdftotext -layout "<input.pdf>" "<output.txt>"`, render relevant pages with `pdftoppm -f <first> -l <last> -scale-to 1800 -png "<input.pdf>" "<output-prefix>"`, and load the resulting images with view_image; claim visual verification only after inspecting the images. Record exhibit number, page, units, period, scenario and footnotes alongside each figure.
   - Build a guidance ledger for revenue, segment growth, operating margin, EPS, capex, FCF, financing, leverage, and capital allocation; retain fiscal year, currency, GAAP/non-GAAP basis, and qualifiers because headline numbers lose meaning without scope.
   - Keep broker forecasts separate from management targets and check whether the broker left its model unchanged; recompute appendix FCF as operating cash flow minus capex before treating the appendix as incorporating new guidance.
   - Compare broker prose, reproduced exhibits and the official release for conflicting fiscal years or margin labels, because editing errors can shift target years or confuse gross and operating margins. Resolve only fields supported by primary evidence and retain remaining discrepancies.
   - Transcribe each project ROI formula and distinguish it from IRR and ROIC, because an annual cash-return proxy may omit timing, replacement capex and residual-value risk. Compare self-funded, vendor-financed and customer-owned-hardware cases using the same capacity and contract term; separate initial-period cash spending from lifetime equipment cost and financing obligations.
   - Reconstruct the funding bridge from remaining capacity and gross investment through customer hardware, supplier funding, prepayments and internal cash generation to residual capital-market need, because the final funding gap is not construction cost. Preserve each percentage’s denominator and distinguish ownership changes from payment deferral.
   - Reproduce the broker target-price formula with its specified EPS period, multiple and net-debt adjustment, because management’s longer-range EPS may not be the target-price earnings basis. Label future guided FCF divided by dated market capitalization as a forward scenario, not current FCF yield, and retain discounting and dilution limitations.
4. Translate fiscal half-years and full years into calendar-month ranges using the issuer fiscal year-end; explicitly resolve shorthand such as “2H28” before describing the cash-flow inflection, because fiscal and calendar halves may differ materially.
5. Compare each target with the prior official outlook and label it new, raised, reaffirmed, or prior-comparison-unverified; do not call every number in today’s release an upgrade because long-range targets may have been announced earlier.
6. Decompose an additional-financing ceiling into equity and gross debt, then quote exclusions and already-completed financing separately; retain debt refinancing, supplier financing, leases, customer-provided hardware, and prepayment conditions because a capital-markets ceiling is not a ceiling on all economic obligations.
7. Explain the FCF bridge using operating cash generation, capex timing, customer prepayments, and equipment funding arrangements; inspect the FCF definition and finance-lease principal treatment because improved reported FCF can partly reflect lower investment or shifted payment timing. Mark any missing bridge components unresolved rather than attributing the entire improvement to operating growth.
8. Copy the issuer definitions of net debt and adjusted EBITDA before assessing a leverage target; retain supplier finance, lease liabilities, cash offsets, and add-backs because differently defined ratios are not directly comparable.
9. Present confirmed targets first, their financing and accounting qualifications second, and investment interpretation third; distinguish an expected self-funded-growth milestone from achieved self-funding because management guidance remains contingent on execution. End with source links and actual reading coverage, and avoid a valuation or trading conclusion without the required price and dilution evidence.
